## 1. Filing calendar

**How dates are published**
- Rule 7 says the BOR "will post in its office and publish the opening and closing dates for filing for each of the 38 townships… No complaint will be accepted after a township's official filing period has closed." (Rules)
- The dates page (https://www.cookcountyboardofreview.com/dates-and-deadlines) links one PDF, TownPDF, which lists townships in **groups**. The page also says: "If you don't see your township listed, please navigate here to pre-file or preregister." The link goes to the portal home.
- FAQ: "The Board of Review opens townships for appeals after the Assessor has assessed each property in the township. Townships have particular filing dates which vary annually. Unfortunately, they are not set in advance." It also says "All townships are opened a minimum of 30 days."
- Pre-registration: "you can pre-register before your township opens for its 30 day appeal window… through the Board's online appeals page… or call (312) 603-5542. The required information is your township, name, address of property and P.I.N." (FAQ)

## 2. Online filing

- **Portal:** "BOR Portal" / "BOR Appeals Portal" at https://appeals.cookcountyboardofreview.com/. Login is at `/Account/LoginPage` and filing is at `/SubmitAppeal/AppealPage1`, both linked from the BOR homepage. The 2026 attorney deck calls the back-end system "Digital Appeals Processing System (DAPS)" (AttyDeck).
- **Account required? No.** HowTo step 2: "Create an online account, log in to your existing account, or submit as a guest." If you file as a guest, the decision is mailed only (see item 7).
- **Steps (HowTo, verbatim summary):**
  1. Find your 14-digit PIN (tax bill or cookcountyassessor.com/address-search).
  2. On cookcountyboardofreview.com, click "Submit an Appeal". Then create an account, log in, or continue as guest.
  3. Select "Submit an Appeal." Choose **"Property Over-Assessed"** from the 'Type of Appeal' drop-down. Enter the PIN, answer whether you are a BOR attorney ("usually 'No'"), and complete the CAPTCHA.
  4. Enter any added PINs, your phone and email, and your **appellant type (owner, beneficiary, executor, etc.)**. Choose whether you want a formal hearing and give the purchase year (or "Older").
  5. Enter comparable properties (optional).
  6. Review, then initial and check the confirmation box and click "Submit." After that, "Upload any supporting documents or evidence (optional)."
- **Account creation fields (username, password, email verification): not confirmed.** The portal is behind a CAPTCHA and the owner portal guide returns 403. The deck mentions "Account lookup by email which sends the user an email with 'Username'" for password resets (AttyDeck).
- **Form name:** "2026 REAL ESTATE ASSESSED VALUATION COMPLAINT", the official Complaint Form (https://www.cookcountyboardofreview.com/sites/g/files/ywwepo261/files/document/file/2026-07/2026%20Complaint%20form.pdf). Rule 5 calls it "the official complaint form… pursuant to 35 ILCS 200/16-105… FACSIMILE FILING OF THE COMPLAINT FORM IS NOT PERMITTED." Rule 9 requires a separate complaint for each property assessment. Present says "A BOR Complaint Number is proof that an appeal has been filed."
- **Other ways to file:** in person at Room 601, 118 N. Clark St., or by mail, where the USPS postmark counts as the filing date (Rule 8). Outreach seminars also take filings (FAQ).

## 3. Who may file

- **Rule 1 (verbatim):** "Only licensed attorneys and individual taxpayers representing themselves ("pro se") may practice before the Board of Review. Individual taxpayers may represent properties titled in their own names pro se, or they may retain an attorney to represent them before the Board. Other than pro se taxpayers, all other parties must be represented before the Board by an attorney, including entities such as corporations, LLCs, condominium associations, and the like. **A person who is not an attorney may not represent a taxpayer before the Board of Review.**" (Rules)
- Present: "If property is owned by a Corporation or an LLC, the property has to be appealed by an Attorney. **A consultant MAY NOT represent a homeowner at the BOR**."
- Rule 16: "All complaints must be signed by the taxpayer, or his/her attorney," and the signature attests that the facts are true.
- The complaint form's appellant checkboxes include "Owner", "Other Liable for Tax", and "Taxing Body or Taxpayer Alleging Underassessment" (Complaint Form PDF). HowTo lists appellant types "owner, beneficiary, executor, etc."
- **Family members and tenants:** no official page addresses them specifically. The form's "Other Liable for Tax" box suggests a non-owner who is liable for the tax (e.g., under a lease) may be an appellant. Whether a relative may file for an owner without being an attorney: **not confirmed** as permitted, and Rule 1's last sentence points against it. The FAQ condo answer is a close analogy: an association president cannot file for the association, but board members "may assist in the compilation of data and evidence."
- Rule 10: "A taxpayer complaint overrides an attorney filing," and later pro se filings on the same PIN control.

## 4. Grounds: uniformity option label

- Present lists the usual pro se grounds as: **"Uniformity"**, under which it says "you can [file] an appeal on the grounds of **Lack of Uniformity**". The others are "Recent Purchase", "Market", "Correction of factual error" and "Special Circumstances" (fire, flood, demolition, uninhabitable). Uniformity guidance: "compare your property to properties located in the same neighborhood and within the same classification code… Building Assessment / Building Square Footage = Building Assessment per square foot."
- **The exact label on the online form or dropdown: not confirmed.** HowTo shows only the appeal type "Property Over-Assessed" plus an optional comparables step. It names no separate grounds dropdown, and I could not reach the portal. The printable 2026 Complaint Form PDF is a fillable form with almost no extractable text, so its grounds checkboxes (if any) were not readable.

## 5. Evidence uploads

- **When to upload:** HowTo says evidence is uploaded after submission ("optional"). Rule 14: "All parties must file their documents no later than the time period set by the Board following the closing of the Township for both desk review and hearing files." TownPDF gives each group's "Evidence Submission Deadline" about 10 days after close (e.g., Group 4: 12/18/2026). FAQ says "up to 5 days prior to your hearing through the online portal or through the clerk's office," which differs from the TownPDF/Rule 14 deadline. **Treat the TownPDF deadline as controlling.** Rule 13 and the deck say late evidence is not accepted, and a hearing does not extend the deadline.
- **Required content:** Rule 14(A): "All parties shall submit a brief as part of their evidence." The brief "shall include a color photo of the front of the subject property taken within the previous twelve months." The FAQ says a picture is "required for the property being appealed… and [is] strongly encouraged for comparable properties. The date the picture was taken should be clearly marked." Rule 18: a purchase within 3 years of the lien date must be disclosed, with sales documents.
- **Comparables:** optional. HowTo says "It is not mandatory to do so, as BOR analysts will prepare comparables regardless." Hearings says supporting documents may include "the property identification numbers of other properties you wish to compare." Present says to use the same neighborhood and the same class code. **Minimum number of comps, distance limits, and required comp fields at the BOR: not confirmed** (none stated). PTAB, by contrast, requires at least 3 comps (item 8).
- **File types/size limits for valuation uploads: not confirmed.** The only stated format rule is for appraisals: "All appraisals shall be submitted in PDF format with pages numbered sequentially" (Rule 19(I)). The deck adds: upload appraisals "as ONE DOCUMENT," label each document with the correct type, and redact personal identity information (SSN/TIN, account numbers: last 4 only). It also notes that "any and all taxpayer submissions are subject to FOIA requests" (AttyDeck).

## 6. Which value is appealed

- FAQ: "When each township reassessment is completed by the Assessor, property owners may appeal the assessment set by the Assessor at the Board. The Board decision sets the final county valuation." "The Board is not bound by the final determination of the Assessor. You are at no disadvantage if the Assessor has granted or denied a request to reduce the assessment."
- The BOR opens a township only after the Assessor finishes it (FAQ: "opens townships for appeals after the Assessor has assessed each property in the township"). On CCAO cal, every township with BOR dates posted has a "Date A-Roll Certified" before the BOR open date. So the appeal is of the Assessor's certified roll value. **"Certified" is not stated in these words on a BOR page**; this is inferred from the calendar.
- If the Assessor has not certified yet, the township is not open at the BOR. The only option is **pre-registration** (FAQ; dates page).
- The 2026 form text reads "OVER ASSESSED by the Assessor of Cook County for the year 2024," which looks like a template or field quirk on the 2026 form. Worth knowing if you are preparing paper filings.

## 7. Decisions, hearings, timing

- **Delivery:** HowTo: "If you logged in: The decision will be emailed and mailed. If you submitted as a guest: The decision will be mailed." The status and 5-year history can be looked up at the BOR Decision Search Portal: https://apps.cookcountyil.gov/BORDecisionSearch/ (search by complaint number, PIN, appellant, attorney or address).
- **Timing:** HowTo: "You will receive a decision within 8-16 weeks after the township's filing deadline." Results26 shows Group 1 (closed 9/1/2026) with a decision date of 10/26/2026, about 8 weeks after close. Groups 2–4 have no decision dates yet (Rev. 11/17/2026). Results25 shows the 2025 session's decisions ran 2/3/2026 to 6/1/2026. HowTo: "Decisions will be reflected in the following year's tax bill."
- **Hearings:**
  - Hearings says "Taxpayers that appeal with the Board of Review have a right to a hearing however it is not mandatory… Taxpayers will not be penalized for waiving." Analysts "use the same analytical process for complaints that request a hearing as those that do not."
  - You request a hearing **at the time of filing**. Present: "The option to request a hearing is made at the time of filing a complaint." HowTo step 4: "Select whether you want a formal hearing."
  - Pro se hearings are **by telephone**. The BOR sends a hearing notice by mail with the date and time, and the Clerk's office contacts taxpayers with phone instructions. Questions go to Allen.manuel@cookcountyil.gov (Hearings; FAQ; Rule 12).
- **Re-Review (Rule 26):** within **3 days** of the date of the decision letter, in writing (online, fax or in person). You must state one of the enumerated grounds, and new arguments are not allowed.

## 8. If denied: PTAB and circuit court

- **PTAB:** "A petition for appeal to PTAB must be filed within **30 days** of the board of county review's written decision, or within 30 days of the written notice of the application of final, adopted township multipliers by the board of review. Your appeal will be rejected if you fail to file on time." (https://ptab.illinois.gov/getStarted.html)
  - Cook-specific: "if the appeal is filed based upon the date the Board of Review transmitted its decision to the county assessor, then you must also supply a copy of the date of transmittal." (https://ptab.illinois.gov/filing.html) Results26/Results25 list each township's "Transmittal Date."
  - Filing is by mail (postmark) or through the eFiling portal, and attorney filers must use eFiling. Faxed or e-mailed appeals are not accepted.
  - Residential appeals seeking a change under $100,000 AV may use comparables or an appraisal. "If you checked **Equity**, submit assessment data on at least three properties in the Grid Analysis (Sec. V)." The grid must be completed in full. (filing.html)
  - PTAB takes evidence not presented to the BOR (35 ILCS 200/16-180 as quoted on https://www.cookcountyboardofreview.com/what-we-do/property-tax-appeal-board).
- **Circuit court:** the FAQ says only that "After first appealing to the Board of Review, you may choose to appeal further to the Cook County Circuit Court or the Property Tax Appeal Board (PTAB)." Rule 27 (Administrative No Change) "preserves the taxpayer's right to appeal to the Cook County Circuit Court or Illinois Property Tax Appeal Board." **Tax-objection mechanics and deadlines: not confirmed** from the allowed sources (they would be in 35 ILCS 200/23 and on Circuit Court or Treasurer pages, which I did not check).
