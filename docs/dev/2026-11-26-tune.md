# Dev log: 2026-11-26, tuning the comparison against Board of Review outcomes

Branch `headless/2026-11-26-tune`, built on `headless/2026-11-25c` (the "building-only comparison" PR), so it should merge after that one. Written by an AI agent (Claude) working unattended.

## Headline

**It generalizes.** One rule beat the current method on tax year 2025, which the search never saw. Pro se AUC went from **0.789 to 0.817 (+0.028, 95% CI +0.025 to +0.030)**, and attorney AUC from 0.751 to 0.777 (+0.026, CI +0.023 to +0.028). On the tuning years the same rule gained +0.031, so about 90% of the gain held up on unseen data. The rule:

- **Nearest similar homes.** Weigh distance 4 per mile instead of 1, and size 1 per 100% difference instead of 4. Drop the basement-type penalty, and keep 30 homes instead of 20. The hard filters (neighborhood, class, size and age bands) are unchanged.
- **Rank instead of gap.** Score a home by how many of those homes have a building assessed lower per sq ft, not by its gap from their median.

That passed the ship gate written down before the holdout ([plan.md](tune-2026-11-26/plan.md)), so the engine now uses it.

- **Tiers.** The evidence report is still offered from +1% above the median with 3+ homes lower. The top tier now needs three quarters of the similar homes assessed lower, not a +5% gap.
- **Quoted rates.** Each report quotes the 2025 rate for one of five groups: the gap first, then the share assessed lower. The rates are 92%, 84%, 67%, 52% and 25%.
- **Shown everywhere, as before:** the gap and the value at the median rate.

## Setup

- **Data.** Board of Review appeals for in-scope homes that ended in a decrease or no change, one row per PIN and tax year. This is the same population as `assessgap backtest`. Win = any reduction.
- **Split.**
  - Tuning: 2023–2024, 368,881 PIN-years; 112,016 pro se appeals scored with 3+ comps.
  - Holdout: 2025, 219,816 PIN-years; 68,092 pro se.
  - The two are cached in separate files (`data/tune/tune.gob`, `holdout.gob`). `tune trial` and `tune signals` refuse a cache that holds 2025.
  - Inside the tuning years, every trial reports 2023 and 2024 separately, and the AUC change in 5 township groups.
- **Fixed population.** Every trial is scored on the same appeals: those the current rule supports with 3+ comps. A trial that finds fewer than 3 comps for one gets a neutral score, so it can't gain by dropping hard cases. Coverage was 100% for every trial.
- **Check.**
  - `tune verify` reproduces the engine backtest's comp count and gap for all 185,872 supported 2024 appeals in the 25c TSV, with 0 mismatches.
  - After shipping, `assessgap backtest -years 2025` reproduces finalist 1's 2025 AUC exactly (0.8166 on the same 68,092 appeals), and `-years 2023,2024` reproduces its tuning AUC (0.8302).
- **Metrics.**
  - Primary: pro se AUC.
  - Also: attorney AUC; the pro se win rate in the top 25% by score, with the share taken; the Spearman correlation of score and % cut among winners; tier calibration (one period's tier rates quoted for another, weighted by tier share).
- **Budget.** 58 configurations, all logged in [trials.tsv](tune-2026-11-26/trials.tsv), failures included: 49 rules in three phases ([phase1](tune-2026-11-26/phase1.jsonl), [phase2](tune-2026-11-26/phase2.jsonl), [phase3](tune-2026-11-26/phase3.jsonl)) and 9 signal models.
- **Speed.** Caching every pool once (55 s) makes a trial about 70 s on all cores, against about 6 minutes for the report-by-report backtest.
- **Pre-registration.** The plan, with the finalist rules and the ship gate, was committed before the first trial. The finalists were committed before the holdout was opened (`212d8a1`, then `fc2d015` added a dry-run mode and the percentile bands, also before the holdout). The holdout ran once, at 01:00 UTC ([holdout-log.tsv](tune-2026-11-26/holdout-log.tsv)).

## Leakage audit

An independent agent read the harness and the SQL, and ran read-only queries. It found **no leak**: nothing from the Board's decision for year Y, and no data from after Y, reaches a year-Y score. Details:

| Input | Known before the decision? |
|---|---|
| Subject's and comps' values for Y | Yes. `COALESCE(certified, mailed)`, the Assessor's values; the Board's columns for Y are never read. `certified` equals the Board's starting value (`bor_appeal.av_before`) within $1 for 100% of 2024 and 99.99% of 2025 appeals, and for 82% of 2023 (the rest are off by about $2, none equal to the Board's result). |
| Prior-year change | Yes. Y's certified value against Y−1's final (Board) value, which is final before Y's sessions. |
| Assessor-stage cut | Yes. Mailed vs certified for Y; both precede the Board. |
| Prior appeals on the PIN | Yes. Tax years Y−2 and Y−1 only. |
| Neighborhood pro se rate | Yes. Appeals from Y−2 and Y−1, grouped by Y's neighborhood. The build's `nbhd_bor_stats` table, which covers the latest years, is never used. |
| Reassessment year | Yes. The Assessor's published triad schedule (South 2023, City 2024, North 2025), confirmed in the data. |
| Data-check flags, search level | Yes, but they inherit today's characteristics (below). |
| Labels (won, cut) | Used only as labels. The fixed population doesn't depend on the outcome. |

Residual bias that this data can't remove:

- **Characteristics are 2026 records for every year.** Only 2025 and 2026 characteristics are published. Suppose an appeal exposed overstated square footage and the Assessor later fixed it. The corrected, smaller area then divides the old, higher building AV, which raises the gap for exactly the homes that won. This likely inflates every method's AUC, most in 2023 and least in 2025. It may slightly favor size-sensitive rules.
- **The current rule saw 2025.** Its distance weight was chosen in the 25c backtest on 2024–2025. That gives the current rule, not the challenger, a head start on the holdout. It was recorded in the plan's addendum before the holdout ran.
- Increases (4,488 across years) are left out, as in the backtest. All rules lose the same rows.

A second agent recomputed the 2025 results in SQL from the per-appeal file and `bor_appeal`, without reading the tool's code:

- every label, filer type and cut matches;
- the `current` column equals the 25c engine's 2025 gaps for all 218,492 appeals;
- all 12 AUCs match to 4 decimals;
- the top-25% rates match.

Its percentile bands 2 and 3 differed by 0.3–0.4 points. The file rounded scores to 6 decimals, which merged the gap tie-break at exactly the 50th percentile. The file now uses full precision. The shipped tiers don't depend on that tie-break, and their rates come from the production backtest.

What helped and what didn't, one change at a time (phase 1):

- **Helped:**
  - distance weight: +0.006 at 2/mile, +0.009 at 4/mile, and +0.009 again at 8/mile, so the gain levels off;
  - the rank (percentile) instead of the gap: +0.012;
  - a lower size weight (2): +0.005;
  - a hard ½-mile limit: +0.003;
  - no basement penalty: +0.003;
  - 30 comps: +0.002.
- **Hurt:**
  - fewer comps (10: −0.012; 15: −0.005);
  - requiring the same basement: −0.013;
  - a higher size weight (8): −0.008;
  - a distance cap of ½ mile: −0.008;
  - dropping the exterior-wall penalty: −0.007;
  - adding central air, basement finish and garage penalties: −0.007;
  - tighter size bands: −0.006;
  - the mean: −0.017;
  - a trimmed mean: −0.003.
- **About zero:** age weight, class weight, land weight, residence-type weight or match, looser bands, and a similarity-weighted median.

The pattern: within the size and age bands, the Board's outcomes follow the nearest homes, and whether most of them are assessed lower, more than how far the home sits from their median. That fits the PTAB reading in the 25c log, where comps on the same block or within ¼ mile were preferred. Distance and rank add up (+0.009 and +0.012 alone, +0.026 together).

Calibration is unchanged by any trial: quoting 2023's tier rates for 2024 is off by about 3.5–3.9 points, weighted.

### Tiers and quoted rates as shipped (production backtest)

The holdout measured the **ranking** (AUC). How to cut the ranking into report tiers was decided after the holdout, in two steps.

1. **First version, pure share bands** (≥ ¾, ½–¾, ¼–½, < ¼ of similar homes lower; strictly lower, so a home assessed the same as its neighbors gains nothing). The final review found a real problem with it: a home the report calls "in line with the median", or even below it, could be offered the PDF and quoted 92% or 81%.
   - Example: PIN 01124070290000, 2025. It is in line, and 29 of 30 homes are "lower" only because their recorded size is 7 sq ft larger.
   - On 2025, strong-but-in-line pro se appeals won 28% (102 appeals), possible-but-in-line 73% (4,922), and possible-below-the-median 70% (1,072).
2. **Shipped: the gap first, then the share.**
   - The evidence report is offered on the same condition as before: +1% or more, and 3+ lower.
   - The top tier needs ¾ lower in place of +5%.
   - The sentence quotes one of five groups. Every group agrees with what the report says about the gap.
   - This changes no AUC. The group rates are descriptive figures for the shipped rule, and the same rule on the tuning years gives similar figures. Nothing was tuned on 2025.

| Group (gap from the median, then share of similar homes lower) | 2023–24 pro se: share / reduction | 2025 pro se: share / reduction | 2025 attorney: share / reduction |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| ≥ +1%, higher than ≥ ¾ of them | 20.0% / 93.0% | 19.7% / **92.2%** | 17.4% / 82.1% |
| ≥ +1%, higher than < ¾ | 19.8% / 82.5% | 19.0% / **84.2%** | 17.5% / 71.5% |
| within ±1% | 13.1% / 64.2% | 13.2% / **67.0%** | 14.1% / 52.8% |
| < −1%, higher than ≥ ¼ | 21.4% / 47.4% | 20.8% / **51.7%** | 21.7% / 40.1% |
| < −1%, higher than < ¼ | 25.8% / 20.4% | 27.3% / **24.6%** | 29.3% / 18.9% |

Sources: [backtest-2025.txt](tune-2026-11-26/backtest-2025.txt) and [backtest-2023-24.txt](tune-2026-11-26/backtest-2023-24.txt).

- **The old tiers on 2025 under the old rule:** ≥+5% 19.4% / 89.2%; +1–5% 17.5% / 85.7%; in line 15.0% / 65.8%; below 48.2% / 38.2%.
- **What the new groups add:** the top group is about as large and wins more often (92.2% vs 89.2%). The share splits the large below-median group into 52% and 25%.
- **Verdicts on 2025 pro se:**
  - strong 92.2% (n 13,356), against 89.2% (13,170) before;
  - possible 84.2% (12,995), against 85.7% (11,936);
  - the evidence report is offered for 38.7% of appeals, against 36.8%.
- **Calibration:** the 2023–24 rates sit within 4.3 points of 2025 in every group. They run lower below the median, so the quoted 2025 rates are the more recent and slightly higher figures.

2. **Fixed:** the website's "how we rank" text still listed the basement and put distance last.
3. **Fixed:** the dev log was missing when it reviewed. It now exists, and the 2023–24 figures have a committed source.
4. **Fixed:** `tune holdout` could spend a finalist's one look on a typo. It now decodes strictly and validates before logging, and the `panic` is gone.
5. **Fixed:** stale golden case comments.
6. **Fixed:** the backtest still called the old cut points "the report's".
7. **Fixed:** compact PDF rows set the caps against the rule above; the type is now centered.
8. **Noted** in methodology: rates are for each group as a whole, and homes near an edge do a little worse or better. In 2025, 50–55% lower won 73%, and 0–5% lower won 16%.
9. **Fixed:**
   - `RankScore` was duplicated; it is now shared.
   - `DefaultParams` was a mutable exported variable; it is now a function returning a copy.
   - `rule25c` is a literal.
   - The optional terms had no tests; they do now.
10. **Recorded here:** the tiering was chosen after the holdout. That doesn't touch any AUC claim.

It confirmed:

## What didn't ship, and why

- **F2 and F3.** F1 beat both on 2025.
- **Pure share-band tiers.** These were the first version of the tiering. The review showed they could contradict the gap shown on the same report (above).
- **A combined score.** F3 is a logistic model; the rules exclude a personal probability. Its gain was smaller than F1's anyway.
- **Context sentences.** One is proposed above for the maintainer.
- **Optional similarity terms.** The new mismatch penalties and the required matches hurt on tuning data. A hard ½-mile limit helped alone, but not on top of 4/mile (#39). The code for them stays, off by default, so the tool can measure them.

## Open questions

1. **Is "nearest" what the Board means by similar?** The data says nearness predicts outcomes best, with size weighted a quarter as much as before. The hard bands still keep comps within ±25% size and ±15 years. If the maintainer prefers that comps read as "most alike" rather than "nearest alike", trial #44 (size 2) was within 0.0006 of #46 on tuning data, but it was not checked on 2025.
2. **The group cut points (¾ and ¼ of similar homes lower) were set by hand,** after the holdout, and not tuned. The 2025 holdout has now been used, so tuning them honestly needs tax year 2026's outcomes. The ranking itself would allow finer groups. The top tier could also require more than +1%.
3. **Characteristics.** Rebuilding the backtest with 2025 characteristics for 2025 appeals (published) would show how much the 2026-record bias inflates AUC. It wasn't done here.
4. **The 40% rise sentence:** ship it, after a 2025 check?
5. **The PDF's compact rows** have only been checked by tests (no PDF viewer here). Please look at a 30-comp page 2 before merging.
